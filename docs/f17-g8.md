## Overview

Feature 17 lets a shipper send freight by asking for a route and a ship date. The system assigns the earliest open flight on that date with room, the shipper pays for it through Feature 5a, hands the cargo over at the terminal, and tracks it to delivery. The shipment is priced by a rate table, checked against the cargo regulations, and tied to its flight by an Air Waybill (AWB) issued when the payment is captured. It has two actors:

- **Shipper** — a signed-in Feature 7 customer who requests cargo space, pays for the booking, cancels it for free before it is loaded, views the Air Waybill, tracks the shipment, reads shipment notices, and files a damage or loss claim on a delivered shipment.
- **Cargo staff** — record the measured weight and volume at the terminal, reject cargo that measures well over its declaration, record load and delivery, move or refund bookings when a flight is cancelled, resolve claims, view the cargo list per flight, and maintain the price list, the weight limits and the prohibited items list.

**Shipper vs passenger.** The shipper is the account holder who sends goods, not someone who flies. Shippers reuse Feature 7's customer account; this feature adds no user type and no sign-in of its own (see Shipper accounts below).

**Decisions already made.**

- Shippers reuse the F7 customer account.
- Hold capacity is checked by weight only; Feature 9 stores no hold volume, so volume is used for pricing only. A full passenger flight leaves little room, because the baggage reserve is taken off the hold limit first: booked passengers, but never fewer than half the sellable seats.
- The rate is chargeable weight × commodity type × distance band.
- Cargo and passenger baggage share the hold weight limit through a baggage reserve plus a live recheck at load time.
- The shipper asks for a route and a ship date picked on a calendar, not a flight. The system assigns the earliest open flight of that day with room, so cargo for the same route and day ends up on the same flight, and one flight carries many kinds of goods (for example a TV order and a refrigerator order).
- Each commodity type has its own weight limits (minimum and maximum per booking, maximum per piece), and a prohibited items list blocks goods that may not fly. The limits and the price list sit on the same commodity type.
- Payment is in the system: the booking holds the weight, the shipper pays through Feature 5a, and refunds go through Feature 5b.
- A booking is confirmed when the payment is captured, with no staff approval step. The Air Waybill number is issued then, from the declared values, and belongs to the booking.
- Staff "accept at terminal" only records the measured weight and volume. If the measured chargeable weight is higher than paid for, the paid charge stays; staff may reject at the terminal only when it is more than 10% above the declared one, and the booking is then refunded.
- Cancelling is free and refunds the full amount. There is no cancellation fee.
- Cargo not loaded at departure becomes rebook\_required.
- When Feature 2 cancels a flight, or diverts it before departure, staff choose per booking: move it to another flight (charge kept) or give a full refund.
- Shipment events create in-app notices for the shipper (confirmed, sent, arrived and so on). No email goes through Feature 13 in this iteration.
- A damage or loss claim is filed by the shipper on a delivered shipment and resolved by staff. The payout is recorded only; no money moves through Feature 5b.
- The database enforces the status moves, the event history and the money arithmetic; the backend owns the assignment, the capacity and the regulation checks.

**Core vs stretch.** Core covers the route request and flight assignment, the regulation checks, the booking with hold and payment, capacity check, rate, Air Waybill, terminal acceptance, tracking, notices, the cargo list per flight and claims. Stretch is special-handling checks (dangerous goods, perishables, live animals).

**Deliberately excluded from scope:** a hold volume check, ULD or pallet build-up, customs, per-piece tracking, splitting one shipment across flights, connecting routes (one booking is one flight), a cancellation fee, paying claims out through the system, email notices, and a shipper-side API for outside companies.

## Data model

Eleven tables carry Feature 17's data; all table names are singular. `cargo_booking` links to Feature 7's `customer`, Feature 2's `route` and `flight` (the flight must belong to the booking's route) through cross-module foreign keys that live in the shared cross-module file, and holds the Feature 5a payment id and the Air Waybill number. A booking has **no status column**: its status is the latest row in `cargo_shipment_event`, so "track the shipment" is a query over stored events. Every event names the event before it, and every move must be a row of `cargo_status_transition`, so two moves made at the same time from the same status can never both be stored.

**Statuses.** `held` → `booked` → `accepted` → `loaded` → `in_transit` → `arrived` → `delivered`, plus `expired`, `cancelled`, `rejected` and `rebook_required`. `held` means the weight is reserved and the booking waits for payment until hold\_expires\_at. `booked` means paid and confirmed, with an Air Waybill number. `accepted` means staff measured the cargo at the terminal. `arrived` means the flight landed and the cargo waits for the consignee; `delivered` follows only from it. `expired` means the hold lapsed or was released without payment. `rebook_required` is set when the booked flight is cancelled or diverted before departure, or when the flight departs without the booking loaded; moving the booking to another flight brings it back to `accepted` if it had been accepted, otherwise `booked`. The shipper creates `held` and cancels; Feature 5a's payment call sets `booked`; staff record `accepted`, `rejected`, `loaded`, `delivered` and `cancelled`. The departure, arrival and flight-change entries come from the flight (see Feature 2 events), and the hold timer sets `expired`.

**Capacity is derived, not stored.** Available cargo weight on a flight is computed on request (see Booking and payment flow); no capacity table exists.

**Seed data.** The migration ships the rows the feature cannot start without: the eight settings, the 24 status transitions, the six prohibited items, the nine commodity types under the four handling classes with their weight limits and three distance bands each (27 rates), and the two stretch conflict pairs. Staff maintain the types, rates and prohibited items afterwards; `seed.ts` adds only demo bookings.

## Shipper accounts

A shipper is a Feature 7 customer. Sign-in, the session exchange and the JWT are exactly as in Feature 7 and Feature 1; Feature 17 adds no Clerk work, no `user_type` and no profile table. `cargo_booking.customer_id` points at the signed-in customer, and every customer route returns only that customer's own bookings.

Shipper company, contact phone and consignee (name, address, phone) are typed on each booking and kept on it, so the same account can ship for different companies. Staff see all bookings. Staff do not book on behalf of a customer in this iteration.

## Booking and payment flow

The shipper asks for space on a route; the system assigns a flight and holds the weight until the shipper pays. The payment captured through Feature 5a confirms the booking and issues the Air Waybill number. Staff then measure the cargo at the terminal.

1. The shipper picks a route and a ship date with a date picker (`GET /cargo/routes`, which lists feature 2's routes with origin, destination and distance). The date picker greys out days with no open flight or no room for the weight, from GET /cargo/routes/:routeId/ship-dates. The shipper picks the day, never the flight.
2. The shipper enters commodity type, pieces, weight, volume, goods description, shipper and consignee details. The form calls `POST /cargo/quotes`, which runs the regulation checks, the flight assignment (below) and the rate calculation, and shows the assigned flight, its departure and the price. It saves nothing.
3. `POST /cargo/bookings` repeats all three inside one transaction, serialised per flight by a transaction-level advisory lock (`pg_advisory_xact_lock` on the flight id), so two shippers cannot take the last kilograms together. It writes the booking with the assigned `flight_id`, its `route_id`, `ship_date`, the rate per kg and minimum charge it was priced with, `booked_at` and `hold_expires_at` = `booked_at` + `hold_minutes` (default 15), and the first `held` event, and returns the booking reference, the flight and the amount due. Held weight counts as committed cargo, so nobody else can take it while the shipper pays. If another shipper took the room after the quote, the booking goes to another flight of the same day when one fits; otherwise it is refused with NO\_FLIGHT\_AVAILABLE and the shipper picks another day. The price does not change, because it depends on the route distance and not on the flight.
4. The shipper pays through Feature 5a's checkout for that booking reference and amount. Feature 5a opens Stripe's checkout for it, the same Pay now hand-off that Feature 3 uses, so Feature 17 shows no card form and holds no card data. Feature 5a authorises the card, then calls the service method `confirmCargoBooking(ref, paymentId, amount)` and captures the money only when it answers 200, as it does for Feature 3's bookings. The call is idempotent per payment id. In one transaction the backend locks the booking row, checks that the amount equals `quoted_charge` and that the hold has not expired, takes the next AWB serial, sets `payment_id`, `awb_number` and `awb_issued_at` together, appends `confirmed` (status `booked`) and writes the shipper's notice. There is no staff approval step. A refused confirm (`HOLD_EXPIRED`, `PAYMENT_MISMATCH`, or a booking that is no longer held) makes Feature 5a void the authorisation, so nothing is charged and no refund is needed. A confirm for a flight that closed after the hold was taken still succeeds, because the weight was reserved before the cutoff.
5. If the shipper abandons the payment, Feature 5a calls `releaseCargoBooking(ref)`; if nobody calls, a job on the simulation clock expires every hold past `hold_expires_at`. Either way an `expired` event is appended and the weight is free again. When the job and a payment arrive together, only one move is stored and the other is refused. The shipper can also cancel a held booking (`DELETE`); nothing was paid, so nothing is refunded.
6. Until the flight loads it, the shipper may cancel a `booked` booking (`DELETE /cargo/bookings/:id`). Cancelling is free: the backend asks Feature 5b for a full refund of `payment_id`, with the source key `cargo:<booking_ref>:cancel` so a retry returns the same refund and never pays twice, and appends `cancelled` with the refund id. After staff accept the cargo at the terminal only staff can cancel, with a note. A booking cannot be edited; to change it, cancel and book again.
7. At the terminal staff weigh and measure the cargo and call `PUT /staff/cargo/bookings/:id/acceptance` with the measured weight and volume. The backend computes the measured chargeable weight and appends `accepted` with the measured values. The booking's quote and the paid charge do not change, even when the measured chargeable weight is higher than declared. If the measured chargeable weight is more than `weight_tolerance_percent` (default 10) above the quoted chargeable weight, staff may instead reject the cargo with a reason (`POST /staff/cargo/bookings/:id/rejection`); that appends `rejected` and refunds the booking in full through Feature 5b. A rejection within the tolerance is refused.
8. At the flight, staff record `loaded`. The backend rechecks the hold (below) and refuses the load if the hold weight limit would be exceeded.
9. When the flight's departure has happened, every loaded booking on it moves to `in_transit` (a `departed` event). A booking still `booked` or `accepted` at that moment gets an `offloaded` event and becomes `rebook_required`. The departure is detected from Feature 2's event or, when there is none, from the flight's `dep_at` passing on the simulation clock.
10. When the flight has landed, in-transit bookings get an `arrived` event at the landing airport and become `arrived`. If Feature 2 diverted the flight after departure, a `diverted` event naming the new airport comes first, the status stays `in_transit`, and the landing airport is the diversion airport. If Feature 2 diverts a flight before it departs, its `booked`, `accepted` and `loaded` bookings get a `diverted` event and become `rebook_required`, like a cancellation, because the cargo would land at the wrong airport. Staff record `delivered` once the consignee has collected the cargo; it follows only from `arrived`.
11. If feature 2 cancels the flight, its `booked`, `accepted` and `loaded` bookings get a `flight_cancelled` event and become `rebook_required`; its `held` bookings expire. Staff choose per booking: move it to another flight of the same route (`PUT /staff/cargo/bookings/:id/flight`, which rechecks capacity there and appends `flight_changed`; the paid charge is kept), or cancel it with a note, which refunds it in full through Feature 5b.
12. After `delivered`, the shipper may file one damage or loss claim within `claim_window_days` (default 14) of the delivery (`POST /cargo/bookings/:id/claims`). Staff open the claims queue and decide: approve with an approved amount (not above the claimed amount) and a note, or reject with a note. The decision is stored and shown to the shipper. It is a record only: no payout or refund moves through Feature 5b.

**Flight assignment rule.** The candidates are the flights of the route whose local service date (`flight.service_date`, the date Feature 3 also searches on) is the ship date the shipper chose, which is today or at most `request_window_days` days ahead (default 30); a 01:00 departure from Bangkok is therefore on its own local date, not the previous day in UTC. A candidate must be open (status scheduled, delayed or holding, and more than `booking_cutoff_hours` before departure), must have available weight for the booking, and must not hold a conflicting handling class (stretch). The booking goes to the candidate with the earliest departure; if two depart together, to the one with more available weight. Cargo for the same route and day therefore fills one flight first and only then the next, and the shipper chooses the day, never the flight. If no candidate fits, the request is refused with `NO_FLIGHT_AVAILABLE`.

**Regulation checks.** Before a quote or a booking is accepted, in this order: (1) the goods description, in lower case, is searched for each active `cargo_prohibited_item` keyword as plain text (not as a pattern), otherwise `PROHIBITED_ITEM`; (2) the booking weight must be between the commodity type's `min_weight_kg` and `max_weight_kg`, otherwise `WEIGHT_OUT_OF_RANGE`; (3) the weight per piece (weight ÷ pieces) must not exceed `max_piece_weight_kg`, otherwise `PIECE_TOO_HEAVY`; (4) dangerous goods need a UN number written as UN and four digits (the database also checks this). The limits and the price list belong to the same commodity type, so a type that is heavier or riskier carries its own limits and its own rate.

**Available cargo weight on a flight.**

```latex
available = holdLimit - passengers \times baggageReserve - committedCargo
```

- `holdLimit` is the hold weight limit of the aircraft assigned to the flight, read from feature 9 (`GET /aircraft/:id/capacity`).
- `passengers` is the larger of the seats held or sold on the flight and the floor. The seats come from Feature 3b's `seat_inventory` (held plus sold over the cabins; a lap infant takes no seat), read through an in-process method. The floor is `baggage_reserve_floor_percent` (default 50) of the aircraft's sellable seats (`total_sellable` from the same Feature 9 call), rounded up.
- `baggageReserve` is `baggage_reserve_kg_per_passenger` in `cargo_setting` (default 15 kg).
- committedCargo is the weight of this flight's bookings in held, booked, accepted, loaded or in\_transit, using the measured weight once accepted and the declared weight before that.

Example: an A320 with a 4,000 kg hold limit and 180 sellable seats, with 150 booked passengers, reserves 2,250 kg for baggage, leaving 1,750 kg for cargo. With only 10 booked passengers the floor of 90 seats applies: 1,350 kg is reserved and 2,650 kg is left for cargo. Passengers who book after the cargo was paid can raise the reserve; paid cargo is never cancelled by itself. If the flight then shows as over its limit, staff move or refund bookings, and the Ship cargo page tells the shipper that cargo space can still change until the cargo is loaded.

**Live recheck at load time.** Before recording `loaded`, feature 17 reads the actual loaded baggage weight for the flight through an in-process method, `loadedBaggageKg(flightId)`, and refuses the load if loaded baggage + loaded cargo + this booking would pass the hold limit. The rev 6 feature list gives the bag weight screen to Feature 10, while the schema review finds the weights in Feature 6's `checked_bag.weight_kg`; the owner of the weights provides the method. If it does not report, the check falls back to the reserve above, so a late baggage feature never blocks cargo. The baggage owner in turn reads feature 17's committed and loaded cargo weight (`cargoWeightOnFlight(flightId)`, also in-process) so its overweight warning includes cargo.

**A flight accepts bookings only when** its status is `scheduled`, `delayed` or `holding` and its current departure time (`dep_at`) is more than `booking_cutoff_hours` after the simulation clock (default 4 hours). Feature 2 gives every flight an aircraft, so there is no unassigned case.

**Feature 2 events.** Feature 17 reads Feature 2's `flight_event` rows in order, in-process, as Feature 3b does: on start-up and from a short job, every event it has not handled yet. Each system row stores the event's id in `flight_event_id` and a unique index ignores a repeat, so no `GET /flights/{id}/events` reconciliation call is needed. `cancelled` and `diverted` are handled as described in the flow. `aircraft_changed` re-runs the capacity calculation with the new aircraft's hold limit; if the committed cargo no longer fits, the flight shows as over its limit by the excess kilograms and staff cancel or move bookings (nothing moves by itself). `delayed` and `rescheduled` need no action, because the cutoff always uses the current `dep_at`. Departure and arrival do not wait for an event: a job on the simulation clock handles flights whose `dep_at` or `arr_at` has passed (or whose status is departed or arrived), because Feature 2 does not yet publish those two events. The job and the event chain make a second run harmless.

## Freight rate

The rate is a rule-based lookup, not a negotiated or dynamic price. Three steps:

- **Chargeable weight.** Light, bulky cargo pays for the space it takes: `volumetric = volume_m3 × volumetric_kg_per_m3` (default 167, the usual 6,000 cm³ per kg), and `chargeable = max(actual kg, volumetric kg)`, rounded up to the next 0.5 kg. It is never below the declared weight.
- **Rate row.** The distance is the booking's route distance from feature 2. The row is the active `cargo_rate` for the booking's commodity type whose band contains that distance (`min_distance_km` ≤ distance < `max_distance_km`; no upper limit when the maximum is empty).
- **Charge.** `freight_charge = max(chargeable × rate_per_kg, min_charge)`, rounded to 2 decimals, in the base currency (feature 4's, USD). The booking copies `rate_per_kg` and `min_charge` when the hold is taken, and the database checks that the stored charge equals this formula.

Volume is used only here. Capacity is checked on actual weight.

**Seed commodity types, rates and limits** (USD per kg, minimum charge 50).

| Commodity type | Class | 0–999 km | 1,000–2,999 km | 3,000 km and over | Min kg | Max kg | Max kg per piece |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General cargo | general | 1.40 | 1.80 | 2.60 | 1 | 1,500 | 100 |
| Garments | general | 1.40 | 1.80 | 2.60 | 1 | 1,500 | 60 |
| Electronics (TVs, computers) | general | 1.60 | 2.10 | 3.00 | 1 | 800 | 80 |
| Appliances (refrigerators, washers) | general | 1.40 | 1.80 | 2.60 | 10 | 1,500 | 120 |
| Machinery | general | 1.50 | 1.90 | 2.70 | 5 | 2,000 | 250 |
| Food and produce | perishable | 2.10 | 2.70 | 3.80 | 1 | 1,000 | 50 |
| Medicine | perishable | 2.60 | 3.30 | 4.60 | 1 | 300 | 30 |
| Dangerous goods | dangerous | 3.00 | 3.80 | 5.20 | 1 | 200 | 50 |
| Live animals | live\_animal | 3.50 | 4.50 | 6.00 | 1 | 500 | 100 |

**Seed prohibited items.** weapons, ammunition, fireworks, narcotics, counterfeit goods, banknotes.

**Worked example.** 120 kg of general cargo, 1.2 m³, on a 1,700 km flight. Volumetric weight is 1.2 × 167 = 200.4 kg, which is more than 120 kg, so chargeable weight is 200.5 kg. The 1,000–2,999 km band gives 1.80, so the charge is 200.5 × 1.80 = 360.90 USD. A dense 300 kg crate of 0.5 m³ on the same flight has volumetric weight 83.5 kg, so it is charged on 300 kg: 540.00 USD.

The quote saved on the booking is the amount due: the shipper pays `quoted_charge`. The booking keeps the rate per kg and minimum charge it was priced with, so staff editing a rate during the hold never changes what the shipper pays, and the charge can always be explained from the booking alone. The charge is not recalculated from the measured values; if the terminal measures more, the paid charge stays. A missing rate row for the commodity and distance rejects the request with `NO_RATE`.

## Shipment tracking, Air Waybill and manifest

**Tracking.** The shipper's tracking page shows the timeline of `cargo_shipment_event` rows for one booking, newest last, each with its simulation-clock time, flight, airport and note; a moved booking shows both flights. Current status is `status_after` of the latest row. A shipper can look up a shipment by booking reference or Air Waybill number, but only their own. Staff see an Arrived list of cargo that has landed and waits for the consignee.

**Air Waybill.** The AWB number is 11 digits: a 3-digit prefix (the airline's number, which Feature 6 already keeps as its bag tag airline number), a 7-digit serial from a database sequence, and a check digit equal to the serial modulo 7 (for example 999-1234567-5; the database checks the format and the digit). It belongs to the booking: `awb_number` and `awb_issued_at` are set together with `payment_id` when the payment is captured, and never change. The Air Waybill is the canonical document tying the shipment to a flight, a shipper and a rate: the shipper views the same record staff see. The page shows shipper, consignee, flight, pieces, declared weight and volume, chargeable weight, rate and charge, all read from the booking, and prints cleanly. The weight and volume measured at the terminal are on the `accepted` entry of the timeline, not on the Air Waybill.

**Cargo manifest per flight.** Staff open a flight and see every booking in `booked`, `accepted`, `loaded`, `in_transit` or `delivered`, one row per Air Waybill: number, shipper, consignee, commodity type, handling class, pieces, declared weight, measured weight (once accepted) and status, with totals and a loaded-vs-booked count. The same data downloads as CSV (`?format=csv`).

**Notices.** Every event that matters to the shipper writes one `cargo_notice` row in the same transaction as the event, so a notice is never lost or sent twice. The shipper sees them in a bell and on a Notices tab in My cargo, newest first, and marks them read. Nothing is emailed; Feature 13 is not used in this iteration.

| Event | Notice to the shipper |
| --- | --- |
| confirmed | Booking confirmed. Air Waybill issued, flight and departure shown |
| expired | Hold expired. The weight was released, nothing was charged |
| accepted | Cargo accepted at the terminal with the measured weight |
| loaded | Cargo loaded on the flight |
| departed | Cargo sent. It is in transit to the destination |
| arrived, diverted | Cargo arrived, or the flight landed at another airport |
| delivered | Cargo delivered to the consignee |
| offloaded | The cargo missed its flight and will be rebooked |
| flight\_cancelled | The flight was cancelled. Staff will move the cargo or refund it |
| flight\_changed | The cargo was moved to another flight |
| cancelled, rejected (with refund) | Refund issued, with the amount |
| claim decision | The claim was approved or rejected, with the note |

## Backend rules (not database constraints)

- **Booking validity:** pieces at least 1; weight and volume above 0; shipper and consignee names and the consignee address not blank; ship\_date not before the simulation date and not later than `request_window_days` ahead; origin and destination come from the route, never typed.
- **Flight assignment:** the booking goes to the open flight of the route on the ship date with the earliest departure (compared with `flight.service_date`, the local date), the date lying inside the `request_window_days` window, that has enough available weight; a tie on departure goes to the flight with more available weight. Done inside the per-flight advisory lock when a hold is taken, repeated at the quote, and the flight is stored on the booking. No fitting flight returns NO\_FLIGHT\_AVAILABLE.
- **Open flight:** status scheduled, delayed or holding, and the current dep\_at more than booking\_cutoff\_hours after the simulation clock, checked when the hold is taken. A payment confirmation for a hold that is still valid is not refused for this reason.
- **Capacity:** available (above) must cover the weight, checked inside the per-flight advisory lock when a hold is taken and at flight change. Held weight counts as committed. Otherwise CAPACITY\_EXCEEDED, with the available kilograms in details. The baggage reserve uses the larger of the seats taken and the floor. Terminal acceptance never refuses for capacity; if the measured weight or later passengers push the flight over its limit, the capacity view shows the excess and the load recheck refuses the load.
- **Regulations:** in this order, a description that contains an active prohibited keyword (plain text, lower case) is refused with PROHIBITED\_ITEM; a weight below min\_weight\_kg or above max\_weight\_kg of the commodity type is WEIGHT\_OUT\_OF\_RANGE; weight divided by pieces above max\_piece\_weight\_kg is PIECE\_TOO\_HEAVY. They run at the quote and again at the booking. Staff edit the limits and the prohibited list; existing bookings keep their own values.
- **Status transitions:** the allowed moves are the rows of `cargo_status_transition` (table below), and the database refuses any event that is not one of them, made by the wrong actor, or that does not follow the booking's latest event. The backend locks the booking row, maps the request to a row, and answers INVALID\_TRANSITION; the hold job and the flight jobs just skip a booking that has already moved. Two moves made at the same time from the same status can never both be stored.
- **Hold and payment:** hold\_expires\_at is booked\_at plus hold\_minutes. The confirm call is idempotent per payment id, and its amount must equal quoted\_charge (PAYMENT\_MISMATCH). An expired hold refuses the confirm with HOLD\_EXPIRED, and any refusal makes Feature 5a void the authorisation, so no captured payment is left behind. A job on the simulation clock expires holds past hold\_expires\_at and frees their weight.
- **Customer limits:** a customer cancels only their own booking, and only while it is held or booked. After staff accept the cargo only staff can cancel, with a note.
- **Refunds:** cancelling a paid booking, a terminal rejection and a staff cancel after a flight cancellation or diversion always refund the paid charge in full through Feature 5b, with a source key per booking and reason, so a retry returns the same refund; there is no cancellation fee. The event is appended with the refund id only after Feature 5b answers; if Feature 5b fails nothing is recorded and the call returns REFUND\_FAILED, so it can be retried without paying twice. A rejection always has a refund id and a note.
- **Terminal acceptance:** only from booked; needs the measured weight and volume; computes the measured chargeable weight with the same rule as the quote (at least the measured weight, in 0.5 kg steps); appends accepted with the measured values. It never changes the booking's quote or the paid charge.
- **Terminal rejection:** only from booked; needs a reason and the measured values. The measured chargeable weight must be more than weight\_tolerance\_percent above the quoted chargeable weight, otherwise TOLERANCE\_NOT\_EXCEEDED. It appends rejected and refunds in full.
- **Loading:** only from accepted; passes the live hold recheck.
- **Departure and arrival** are system-only and come from the flight job: in\_transit only from loaded (on departure), arrived only from in\_transit, and delivered only from arrived. The landing airport is on the arrived row, and is the diversion airport if feature 2 diverted the flight after departure.
- **Diversion before departure:** moves booked, accepted and loaded bookings to rebook\_required, like a cancellation; a diversion in flight keeps in\_transit.
- **Feature 2 events:** read in order in-process, applied once per flight\_event\_id (unique index); aircraft\_changed only recomputes capacity and never moves a booking. A cancelled flight expires its held bookings.
- **Rebooking:** the new flight must belong to the same route (the database checks flight and route together) and depart after the simulation clock, and passes the open-flight and capacity rules. The status returns to accepted if the booking has an accepted event, otherwise booked. The paid charge and the Air Waybill number are kept, and the history keeps the flight each event happened on.
- **Claims:** only for the booking's customer, on a booking in delivered, within claim\_window\_days of the delivered event, one per booking (CLAIM\_NOT\_ALLOWED). The description is required, claimed\_amount is above 0, and pieces\_affected is between 1 and pieces. A decision is written once (CLAIM\_ALREADY\_DECIDED); an approval needs an approved\_amount above 0 and not above claimed\_amount; both outcomes need a note. No call to Feature 5b is made.
- **Notices:** every event in the notice table (under Tracking) writes one cargo\_notice row in the same transaction as the event, and the database allows one notice per event and per claim decision. A notice is never edited or deleted; only read\_at is set, by the booking's customer. No other module is called.
- **Dangerous goods** (handling class dangerous) require a UN number on the booking, written as UN and four digits; the booking carries the commodity type's handling class, held to it by a composite foreign key.
- **Stretch, handling conflicts:** when a hold is taken and at flight change, refuse the booking when its handling class conflicts (cargo\_handling\_conflict) with any held, booked, accepted or loaded booking on the flight, naming the booking reference it conflicts with (HANDLING\_CONFLICT).
- **Rates:** bands of one commodity do not overlap (database constraint); setting a rate, a limit or a setting value is validated by the backend. Rates and limits are edited in place, which is safe because bookings keep their own copy of the rate.
- **Immutability:** a database trigger refuses update, delete and truncate on cargo\_shipment\_event; the backend has no update or delete path for cargo\_claim or cargo\_claim\_decision either.
- **Times:** every booked\_at, hold\_expires\_at, awb\_issued\_at, occurred\_at, filed\_at, decided\_at and notified\_at comes from the simulation clock, never the system clock. created\_at and updated\_at are real time, as everywhere in the repo.

-- The database holds the checks that are about rows: the status moves and the event chain, the measured values, the

-- the regulation keyword and weight checks, the hold timer, the weight tolerance, the claim window and the refund

-- Commodity types, rates and keywords ---------------------------------------------------------------------

-- The goods types a shipper picks from, each with its regulation limits.
CREATE TABLE cargo_commodity_type (
  id                  UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  code                TEXT NOT NULL UNIQUE,                     -- e.g. 'GEN'
  name                TEXT NOT NULL,
  handling_class      TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (handling_class IN ('general', 'perishable', 'dangerous', 'live_animal')),
  min_weight_kg       NUMERIC(10, 1) NOT NULL DEFAULT 1 CHECK (min_weight_kg > 0),

-- Bookings --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

-- One booking is one flight of its route; the backend assigns flight_id and changes it only when staff move the booking,
-- always to a flight of the same route. Status is the latest shipment event (cargo_booking_current).
-- handling_class is the commodity type's, held to it by the foreign key, so a dangerous goods booking needs a UN number.
-- The quote columns are written once at the hold: the charge is the chargeable weight (at least the declared weight,
-- in steps of 0.5 kg) times the copied rate, but never less than the copied minimum charge.
-- The Air Waybill number is issued when feature 5a captures the payment: payment_id, awb_number and awb_issued_at are
-- set together and never change. awb_number is the airline's 3-digit number, a 7-digit serial from air_waybill_serial_seq
-- and a check digit equal to the serial modulo 7.
CREATE SEQUENCE air_waybill_serial_seq START 1000000 MINVALUE 1000000 MAXVALUE 9999999 NO CYCLE;

CREATE TABLE cargo_booking (
  id                   UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),

  route_id             UUID NOT NULL,                          -- feature 2's route (cross-module FK)
  flight_id            UUID NOT NULL,                          -- feature 2's flight of that route (cross-module FK)
  ship_date            DATE NOT NULL,                          -- the day the shipper chose (a local date), equal to the flight's service_date
  commodity_type_id    UUID NOT NULL,
  handling_class       TEXT NOT NULL,
  goods_description    TEXT NOT NULL,
  un_number            TEXT CHECK (un_number ~ '^UN[0-9]{4}$'),
  pieces               INTEGER NOT NULL CHECK (pieces > 0),
  declared_weight_kg   NUMERIC(10, 1) NOT NULL CHECK (declared_weight_kg > 0),

  shipper_name         TEXT NOT NULL,
  shipper_phone        TEXT,
  consignee_name       TEXT NOT NULL,
  consignee_address    TEXT NOT NULL,
  consignee_phone      TEXT,
  distance_km          INTEGER NOT NULL CHECK (distance_km > 0), -- the route's distance when quoted
  quoted_chargeable_kg NUMERIC(10, 1) NOT NULL,
  quoted_rate_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES cargo_rate (id),
  rate_per_kg          NUMERIC(10, 2) NOT NULL CHECK (rate_per_kg > 0),
  min_charge           NUMERIC(10, 2) NOT NULL CHECK (min_charge >= 0),
  quoted_charge        NUMERIC(12, 2) NOT NULL,                -- the amount due and, once paid, the amount kept

-- Shipment events -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

-- Append-only history. Each event names the event before it (previous_event_id) and that event's status
-- (status_before), and an event can be followed only once, so two moves made at the same time from the same status
-- cannot both be stored: the second insert fails and the backend reloads the booking. The move itself must be in
-- cargo_status_transition. The latest row (by seq, which follows the chain) is the current status.
-- flight_id is the flight the booking was on when it happened, so a moved booking keeps the history of both flights.
-- System rows from feature 2 carry its flight_event_id, so a repeated event is a no-op. accepted and rejected rows carry
-- the measured values; cancelled and rejected rows carry feature 5b's refund_id when money went back.
CREATE TABLE cargo_shipment_event (
  seq                    BIGINT GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY UNIQUE,
  id                     UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  cargo_booking_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES cargo_booking (id),
  previous_event_id      UUID,
  status_before          TEXT,
  event_type             TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (event_type IN (
                           'held', 'confirmed', 'expired', 'accepted', 'rejected', 'cancelled', 'loaded', 'departed',
                           'arrived', 'diverted', 'delivered', 'offloaded', 'flight_cancelled', 'flight_changed')),
  status_after           TEXT NOT NULL,
  flight_id              UUID NOT NULL,                        -- feature 2's flight (cross-module FK)
  airport_code           TEXT,                                 -- feature 2's airport (cross-module FK)
  note                   TEXT CHECK (btrim(note) <> ''),
  actor_type             TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (actor_type IN ('customer', 'staff', 'system')),
  recorded_by_staff_id   UUID REFERENCES staff (id),
  flight_event_id        UUID,                                 -- feature 2's flight_event (cross-module FK)
  refund_id              UUID,                                 -- feature 5b's refund
  measured_weight_kg     NUMERIC(10, 1) CHECK (measured_weight_kg > 0),
  measured_volume_m3     NUMERIC(8, 3) CHECK (measured_volume_m3 > 0),
  measured_chargeable_kg NUMERIC(10, 1),
  occurred_at            TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  created_at             TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  UNIQUE (id, cargo_booking_id),
  UNIQUE (id, cargo_booking_id, status_after),
  -- An event is followed once; the foreign key below fixes the booking and status, so the triple means "once per event".
  CONSTRAINT cargo_shipment_event_followed_once UNIQUE (previous_event_id, cargo_booking_id, status_before),
  CONSTRAINT cargo_shipment_event_previous_fk FOREIGN KEY (previous_event_id, cargo_booking_id, status_before)
    REFERENCES cargo_shipment_event (id, cargo_booking_id, status_after),
  CONSTRAINT cargo_shipment_event_transition_fk FOREIGN KEY (status_before, event_type, status_after, actor_type)
    REFERENCES cargo_status_transition (status_before, event_type, status_after, actor_type),
  CHECK ((previous_event_id IS NULL) = (status_before IS NULL)),
  CHECK ((previous_event_id IS NULL) = (event_type = 'held')),
  CHECK (event_type <> 'held' OR (status_after = 'held' AND actor_type = 'customer')),
  CHECK ((actor_type = 'staff') = (recorded_by_staff_id IS NOT NULL)),
  CHECK (flight_event_id IS NULL OR actor_type = 'system'),
  CHECK (num_nonnulls(measured_weight_kg, measured_volume_m3, measured_chargeable_kg)
         = CASE WHEN event_type IN ('accepted', 'rejected') THEN 3 ELSE 0 END),
  CHECK (measured_chargeable_kg >= measured_weight_kg AND measured_chargeable_kg * 2 = trunc(measured_chargeable_kg * 2)),
  CHECK (refund_id IS NULL OR event_type IN ('cancelled', 'rejected')),
  CHECK (event_type <> 'rejected' OR (refund_id IS NOT NULL AND note IS NOT NULL)),
  CHECK (NOT (event_type = 'cancelled' AND actor_type = 'staff') OR note IS NOT NULL)
);

CREATE UNIQUE INDEX cargo_shipment_event_first_uq ON cargo_shipment_event (cargo_booking_id) WHERE previous_event_id IS NULL;
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX cargo_shipment_event_flight_event_uq
  ON cargo_shipment_event (cargo_booking_id, flight_event_id) WHERE flight_event_id IS NOT NULL;
-- Accepted at the terminal once; a moved booking returns to accepted through a flight_changed row.
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX cargo_shipment_event_accepted_uq ON cargo_shipment_event (cargo_booking_id) WHERE event_type = 'accepted';
CREATE INDEX cargo_shipment_event_booking_idx ON cargo_shipment_event (cargo_booking_id, seq DESC);
CREATE INDEX cargo_shipment_event_flight_idx ON cargo_shipment_event (flight_id);

-- The shared forbid_mutation() trigger function, as on flight_event and audit_log.
CREATE TRIGGER cargo_shipment_event_append_only
  BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE ON cargo_shipment_event
  FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION forbid_mutation();

CREATE TRIGGER cargo_shipment_event_no_truncate
  BEFORE TRUNCATE ON cargo_shipment_event
  FOR EACH STATEMENT EXECUTE FUNCTION forbid_mutation();

-- Current status of every booking: the latest event's status_after.
CREATE VIEW cargo_booking_current AS
SELECT DISTINCT ON (cargo_booking_id)
       cargo_booking_id, status_after AS status, occurred_at AS status_at
FROM cargo_shipment_event
ORDER BY cargo_booking_id, seq DESC;

**Decisions made, defaults still to agree**

- Freight is paid in the system through Feature 5a; refunds go through Feature 5b.
- The shipper asks for a route and a ship date picked on a calendar, not a flight. The system assigns the earliest open flight of the route on that day that has room. Cargo for the same route and day therefore goes together, and it fills one flight before the next.
- Capacity on a passenger flight is the aircraft's hold limit minus the baggage reserve minus the cargo already committed. The reserve counts the booked passengers, but never fewer than half the sellable seats, so a full flight has little room and an empty one still keeps space for bags. Cargo of different commodity types shares a flight.
- Every commodity type has a price list (rate by distance band) and its own regulation limits (minimum and maximum weight, maximum weight per piece); a prohibited-item list refuses goods by keyword. Staff edit both.
- Nine commodity types sit under the four handling classes (general, perishable, dangerous, live animals); the types and rates ship with the migration.
- The shipper is told about each step by an in-app notice (bell and Notices tab); nothing is emailed.
- A booking is confirmed on payment with no staff approval; the Air Waybill number is issued then and belongs to the booking.
- The paid charge stays when the measured chargeable weight is higher; staff may reject at the terminal only above 10% and the booking is refunded.
- Cancelling has no fee and refunds in full.
- Cargo not loaded at departure becomes rebook\_required, as does cargo on a flight that is cancelled or diverted before departure.
- When a flight is cancelled or diverted before departure, staff choose per booking: move it (charge kept) or refund in full.
- Landed cargo is `arrived` and is delivered only from there.
- The database enforces the status moves, the event chain, the measured values, the charge arithmetic and the notice links; the backend owns the flight assignment, capacity and regulation checks.
- Damage or loss claims are filed on delivered shipments and resolved by staff; the payout is recorded only.
